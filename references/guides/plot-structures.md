# Plot Structure Templates

Common story-structure templates you can use to plan a novel's chapters.

## Three-Act Structure

The classic story structure, suited to most short-to-medium works.

### Act 1 — Setup (≈25%)

| Chapter | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Ch. 1 | Introduce the protagonist and their ordinary life (status quo) |
| Ch. 2 | The Inciting Incident — the event that shatters the status quo |
| Ch. 3 | The protagonist resists the call, then finally decides to act |

**Sample Act 1 hooks:**
- "The door opened, and the man who stepped through was someone who'd been dead for three years."
- "The letter she received was signed with her own name."

### Act 2 — Confrontation (≈50%)

| Chapter | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Ch. 4–5 | The protagonist enters a new world and faces its challenges |
| Ch. 6 | The Midpoint — a major turn of events / revelation |
| Ch. 7–8 | Difficulties escalate; allies may betray |

**Sample Act 2 hooks:**
- "The mentor was the mastermind all along."
- "The one true ally has gone missing."

### Act 3 — Resolution (≈25%)

| Chapter | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Ch. 9 | Everything seems lost; the lowest point (dark night of the soul) |
| Ch. 10 | The Climax — the final confrontation |
| Final chapter | The ending — establishing a new normal |

## The Hero's Journey

Mythologist Joseph Campbell's classic structure, ideal for adventure and fantasy.

### Stages

| Stage | Chapter | Content |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 1. The Ordinary World | Ch. 1 | Introduce the protagonist's everyday life |
| 2. The Call to Adventure | Ch. 1–2 | The inciting incident occurs |
| 3. Refusal of the Call | Ch. 2 | The protagonist hesitates |
| 4. Meeting the Mentor | Ch. 2–3 | Receives guidance / equipment |
| 5. Crossing the Threshold | Ch. 3 | Leaves the comfort zone |
| 6. Tests, Allies, Enemies | Ch. 4–5 | Exploration of the new world |
| 7. Approach to the Inmost Cave | Ch. 5–6 | Preparing for the great challenge |
| 8. The Ordeal | Ch. 6–7 | Brush with death / major failure |
| 9. The Reward | Ch. 7–8 | Gains power / knowledge |
| 10. The Road Back | Ch. 8 | Obstacles on the return |
| 11. Resurrection | Ch. 9 | The final test / transformation |
| 12. Return with the Elixir | Ch. 10 | Returns home bearing what was won |

## Mystery Structure

For detective, procedural, and thriller stories.

### Act 1 — The Mystery Appears

| Chapter | Content | Suspense hook |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Ch. 1 | A body is found / the event occurs | Who did it? |
| Ch. 2 | The detective takes the case | Why is this case special? |
| Ch. 3 | Initial investigation turns up clues | Who do the clues point to? |

### Act 2 — The Investigation Deepens

| Chapter | Content | Suspense hook |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Ch. 4 | Suspects are interrogated; each looks guilty | Who's lying? |
| Ch. 5 | A new clue surfaces, pointing in an unexpected direction | Were we wrong from the start? |
| Ch. 6 | A second incident reveals a pattern | Is this a serial case? |
| Ch. 7 | The detective falls into danger | Will the detective be next? |

### Act 3 — The Truth Surfaces

| Chapter | Content | Suspense hook |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Ch. 8 | A major breakthrough / reversal | Is someone we trust implicated? |
| Ch. 9 | The final confrontation | What's the truth, and what does it cost? |
| Ch. 10 | The case resolves, with threads left over | Is it really over? |

## Romance Structure

For love stories.

### Act 1 — The Meeting

| Chapter | Content | Emotional beat |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| Ch. 1 | Introduce lead A, their situation/problem | Reader empathizes with A |
| Ch. 2 | Introduce lead B, their situation/problem | Reader empathizes with B |
| Ch. 3 | A and B meet (first impressions are poor) | They're meant to be — but there's friction now |

### Act 2 — The Development

| Chapter | Content | Emotional beat |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| Ch. 4–5 | Forced together; they come to know each other | Discovering what draws them |
| Ch. 6 | Attraction grows; an intimate moment | Drawing closer |
| Ch. 7–8 | Misunderstanding / secret / obstacle arrives | Pushing apart |
| Ch. 9 | Crisis; the relationship seems to break | It looks hopeless |

### Act 3 — The Reconciliation

| Chapter | Content | Emotional beat |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| Ch. 10 | Realizing the truth of their love; overcoming the obstacles | The climax |
| Final chapter | Together; a new life shown | Resolution |

## Thriller / Action Structure

For fast-paced, high-tension stories.

### Short-Form Structure (5–6 chapters)

```
Ch. 1: The crisis arrives → hook: the protagonist is hunted or threatened
Ch. 2: Plan of attack → hook: the plan fails, things get worse
Ch. 3: The chase / confrontation → hook: backed into a corner
Ch. 4: A chance to turn the tables → hook: new hope, but the clock is running
Ch. 5: The final confrontation → hook: a life-or-death moment
Ch. 6: The ending → show the consequences
```

### Key Traits

- Fast pace; action in every chapter
- High event density
- Sustained time pressure
- Escalating suspense intensity

---

## Twist-Based Structure

For psychological thrillers and mysteries built on reversals.

### Chapter Distribution

```
Ch. 1–2: Establish the "true" situation
Ch. 3–4: Doubts emerge, but stay subtle
Ch. 5–6: First small twist (reinterpretation)
Ch. 7–8: Second twist (the reversal flips again)
Ch. 9–10: Final twist (everything is overturned)
```

### Key Principles

- Plant apparently innocent clues early on
- Every twist must follow logically (re-reading shows the trail was there)
- Avoid Deus Ex Machina

## Multi-POV / Multi-Thread Narrative

For complex plots with several protagonists.

### Crosscutting Pattern

```
Ch. 1: Protagonist A's story
Ch. 2: Protagonist B's story
Ch. 3: Protagonist A's story (advances)
Ch. 4: Protagonist B's story (advances)
Ch. 5: The threads converge
...
```

### Timeline Pattern

```
Ch. 1: Present (time A)
Ch. 2: Past (time A − 5 years)
Ch. 3: Present (time A + 1 day)
Ch. 4: Past (time A − 5 years + 1 month)
...
```

### Convergence Rules

- All threads must eventually meet
- Events that seemed unrelated early on turn out to be connected
- The convergence produces an "aha, that's what it meant" effect

## Quick Structures for Short Fiction

### 3-Chapter Micro-Structure

```
Ch. 1: Inciting incident + decision to act
Ch. 2: Attempt + failure + escalation
Ch. 3: Final attempt + success/failure + resolution
```

### 5-Chapter Standard Structure

```
Ch. 1: Status quo + inciting incident
Ch. 2: Refusal + crossing the threshold
Ch. 3: Challenges + allies/enemies
Ch. 4: Low point + awakening
Ch. 5: Climax + resolution
```

## The Chapter-Level Plot Template

### Internal Chapter Rhythm

```
Opening (10%): hook + connective tissue to prior context
Development (60%): events advance + conflict
Climax (20%): this chapter's highest point
Close (10%): end-of-chapter hook + groundwork for the next chapter
```

### The Ineffective Chapter

```
Opening: long-winded setup / background exposition
Middle: running through daily activities / a log of aimless dialogue
Ending: a flat close with no suspense
```

## Choosing a Structure

| Story type | Recommended structure | Chapter count |
|------------|-----------------------|---------------|
| Adventure / Fantasy | Hero's Journey | 8–12 |
| Detective / Mystery | Mystery Structure | 8–10 |
| Romance | Romance Structure | 6–10 |
| Action / Thriller | Thriller Structure | 5–8 |
| Psychological / Twist | Twist-Based Structure | 6–8 |
| Multi-protagonist | Multi-POV Narrative | 10–15 |
| Flash / micro-fiction | 3-Chapter Structure | 3 |
