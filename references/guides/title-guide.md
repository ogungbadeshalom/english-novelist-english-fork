# Title Craft Guide

A title is a novel's first impression. A good title plants a seed of curiosity in the reader's mind and drives clicks and sharing.

Use this guide during the Phase 1, Layer 3 title-generation step. Based on the story's genre, central conflict, characters, and themes, generate candidate titles that follow these rules.

---

## Genre-to-Style Mapping

Different genres lean toward different title styles. Determine the genre first, then match the corresponding style rules.

| Genre | Style traits | Common patterns | Examples |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|----------|
| Mystery / Detective | Suspense, unresolved puzzle | "the mystery of X," "the missing X," "the Nth X" | *The [Numbered] Victim*, *The Vanishing Riddle* |
| Romance | Poetic, warm | Imagery, short evocative phrases, contrast | *The Taste of Autumn*, *A Late Love, Met in Time* |
| Fantasy | Grand, mythic | Chronicle/epic words, saga names | *The Storm Chronicle*, *Ballad of the Deep Vale* |
| Science Fiction | Futuristic, philosophical | Technical imagery, paradox structures | *Echoes from Deep Space*, *The Final Question* |
| Historical | Weighty, of an era | Years, places, anchoring events | *The Twelve Hours of [City]*, *Annals of a Falling Dynasty* |
| Contemporary | Grounded, everyday | Colloquial phrasing, contrast | *It's All Good*, *City Smoke and Stoves* |
| Horror / Thriller | Oppressive, taboo | Suggestion, incomplete phrasing | *Never Look Back*, *The Thirteenth Floor* |

> For cross-genre stories (e.g. "mystery + romance"), prioritize the style of the genre that carries the central conflict, while weaving in elements of the secondary genre.

## Title-Craft Techniques

When generating candidate titles, apply the techniques below — and make sure each candidate uses a different one.

### 1. Extract the Core Conflict

Distill keywords from the story's central conflict and combine them into a title that points straight at the heart of the problem.

**Examples:**
> [Mystery] The story revolves around tracing a string of disappearances → *The Thread of the Missing*
> [Historical] The core conflict is court power struggle → *The Unfinished Game*
> [Sci-Fi] An AI's awakening and its opposition to humanity → *The Creator's Dilemma*

**Key requirements:**
- The title's keywords must relate directly to the story's central conflict
- Readers should get a rough sense of the story's direction from the title alone

### 2. Name the Protagonist

Build the title around the protagonist's defining trait, identity, or name, forging a strong link between title and character.

**Examples:**
> [Contemporary] The protagonist runs a late-night diner → *The Night Cook*
> [Fantasy] The protagonist is an exiled prince → *Return of the Exile*
> [Romance] The protagonist is an amnesiac girl → *Her Stranger's Diary*

**Key requirements:**
- Highlight the protagonist's most distinctive quality
- Avoid using the full name outright (too much like a biography)

### 3. Image as Metaphor

Use a core image to suggest the theme, leaving room for interpretation. The title itself becomes a symbol readers can savor repeatedly.

**Examples:**
> [Mystery] A story wound through with truth and lies → *The Lighthouse in the Fog*
> [Romance] A love that crosses time → *The Letter Repeatedly Folded*
> [Sci-Fi] Exploring the boundary between consciousness and reality → *Mirror Within Mirror*

**Key requirements:**
- The image has a metaphorical relation to the theme — but not a one-to-one match
- Readers can re-interpret the title anew as they read

### 4. Juxtapose Contradictions

Create a cognitive contrast on the title's surface, setting seemingly contradictory elements side by side to spark curiosity.

**Examples:**
> [Horror] Gentleness and dread coexisting → *Her Last Smile*
> [Historical] Splendor against decay → *The Crack in a Golden Age*
> [Contemporary] The ordinary versus the extraordinary → *Big Day for Nobody*

**Key requirements:**
- Two seemingly contradictory elements placed together
- Generate a "why would that be?" curiosity

### 5. Withhold the Answer

The title doesn't reveal the whole truth — it leaves room to imagine, planting a "what is it, really?" question.

**Examples:**
> [Mystery] Doesn't say what the case involves → *The Third Letter*
> [Fantasy] Doesn't say where the place is → *The Door and What Lies Beyond*
> [Romance] Doesn't say what the promise entails → *That Winter's Promise*

**Key requirements:**
- The title points at something specific without explaining it
- The gap left open *is* the suspense

## Quality Standards

Every candidate title must meet these standards:

### Must Satisfy

- **Length:** 2–8 words is the mainstream; special genres may stretch to 10
- **Originality:** avoid titles that echo well-known published works
- **Readability:** rolls off the tongue; easy to remember and share
- **Diversity:** within the same batch, each candidate uses a different technique

### Must Avoid

- **AI-flavor stock structures:** forbidden clichés like "The Way of X," "The Awakening of X," "The Era of X," "The Rise of X"
- **Too vague:** one-word titles like "Fate," "Choice," "Truth" with no specific referent
- **Too on the nose:** titles that spoil the ending or the central twist
- **Clickbait:** headline-style hooks like "Shocking," "Unbelievable," "Wait Till You Hear This"
